Laplace complete monotonicity and theta-moment audit
Independent bounded research batch, 2026-11-11.
Status: unconditional partial lemmas and an exact missing positivity
condition. No claim to establish RH.

Subsequent literature update: the xi-specific first ratio inequality
identified below IS resolved by Newman's 1991 theorem that the theta
potential derivative is convex. See 2026-11-11-ghs-first-ratio.txt for
the elementary one-site GHS deduction and the next unresolved bound.
The generic-kernel counterexample and the full Hankel gap below remain
valid; the earlier wording of the first xi-specific gap is superseded.

Definitions
-----------
Write F(w)=xi(1/2+w)/xi(1/2), kappa(w)=log F(w) for real w, and

    L(w)=kappa'(w)/w^2,  w>0.

The paper's transform gives, initially for w>1/2,

    L(w)=integral_0^infinity [-g(t)] exp(-w*t) dt.

The unconditional zero expansion gives |g(t)|<=C*(exp(t/2)+1).
Also xi(1/2+w)>0 for real w, by its positive theta integral.

1. What the positive theta kernel DOES prove
------------------------------------------
Let A(u)>0 be the theta kernel specified in the companion note. Then

    F(w)=E[exp(w*X)]

for a symmetric real random variable X having density proportional
to A(|x|). All required exponential moments exist. Hence

    kappa'(w)=E_w[X]>0 for w>0,
    kappa''(w)=Var_w(X)>0,

where E_w denotes exponential tilt by exp(wX). In particular L(w)>0
for all w>0. Every derivative of F is positive for w>0 as well.

The raw even moments b_n=E[X^(2n)]=xi^(2n)(1/2)/xi(1/2) satisfy
both families of Stieltjes Hankel inequalities:

    [b_(i+j)] >= 0,   [b_(i+j+1)] >= 0.

Indeed their quadratic forms are integrals of a squared polynomial
in X^2, optionally multiplied by X^2. These matrices are strictly
positive definite for nonzero finite polynomials since A is positive
on every positive interval.

2. The first ratio inequality is not automatic
--------------------------------------------
Complete monotonicity of L first requires

    -L'(w)=[2*kappa'(w)-w*kappa''(w)]/w^3 >= 0.

Positivity of a symmetric theta-type integral, considered just as
positivity of its mixing measure, supplies no such general theorem.
An exact counterexample to that proposed GENERAL implication is

    P(X=0)=99/100, P(X=1)=P(X=-1)=1/200,
    F_*(w)=99/100+cosh(w)/100.

At w_*=log(100), put kappa_*=log F_*. Then

    cosh(w_*)=10001/200, sinh(w_*)=9999/200,
    kappa_*'(w_*)=9999/29801,
    kappa_*''(w_*)=198059800/888099601.

The ratio kappa_*''/kappa_*'=198059800/297980199 exceeds 1/2.
Since log(100)>4, w_* kappa_*''>2 kappa_*', so L_*'(w_*)>0.
This does not refute the xi-specific inequality. It proves that an
additional quantitative property of its particular kernel is needed.

The obstruction persists for a smooth strictly positive density:
convolve this law with a centered Gaussian of variance 1/100.
This adds w^2/200 to kappa_*. Thus w*kappa''-2*kappa' changes by
-w/100. At w_*, the original difference exceeds 2/15, since the
displayed ratio exceeds 3/5, kappa_*'>1/3, and w_*>4. Meanwhile
w_*/100<1/20. The resulting difference remains positive.

The raw-moment/cumulant gap already appears in degree four. RH
would imply

    kappa''''(0)=b_2-3*b_1^2 < 0,

because it equals -6*sum_gamma m_gamma/gamma^4 under real-zero
support. Positive mixing measures need not have this sign: the
three-point example has b_1=b_2=1/100 and positive fourth cumulant
97/10000. Raw Hankel positivity is therefore not the desired
logarithmic-derivative positivity.

3. An unconditional all-orders LOCAL lemma
----------------------------------------
Nevertheless L is completely monotone on some initial interval
(0,epsilon), with epsilon>0, WITHOUT RH. This is a local statement
and does not mean that L is a Laplace transform on the whole axis.

Proof. By symmetry and xi(1/2)!=0,

    L(w)=M/w+R(w),  M=xi''(1/2)/xi(1/2)>0,

where R is analytic at zero. Choose r>0 so R is analytic on a
neighborhood of the closed disk |w|<=r, and let B bound |R| there.
Cauchy's estimate, centered at real 0<w<r, gives

    |R^(n)(w)| <= n!*B/(r-w)^n.

Consequently

    (-1)^n L^(n)(w)
      >= n!/w^(n+1) * [M-B*w*(w/(r-w))^n] > 0

for EVERY n>=0 whenever 0<w<min(r/2,M/B); interpret M/B as infinity
if B=0. This proves the assertion. The existence of r and finite B
does not presume anything about zeros outside this local disk.

Thus even verifying all derivative signs near w=0 cannot by itself
settle the global question.

4. Each FIXED derivative also has the right eventual sign
-------------------------------------------------------
The elementary logarithmic-derivative formula, Stirling expansion,
and the absolutely convergent Euler product for large w give

    L(w)=[log(w/(2*pi))]/(2*w^2)+O(w^(-3)).

The differentiated expansions are valid to each fixed order n and
yield

    (-1)^n L^(n)(w)
     = (n+1)!/(2*w^(n+2))
       *[log(w/(2*pi))-(H_(n+1)-1)+O_n(1/w)].

Therefore for every fixed n its complete-monotonicity inequality
holds for all sufficiently large w. The threshold can depend on n.
This supplies neither a uniform threshold for all n nor the missing
global inequalities in between.

5. Exact Hankel target at ONE Laplace parameter
----------------------------------------------
Fix any w0>1/2 and define

    a_n=(-1)^n L^(n)(w0)
       =integral t^n [-g(t)] exp(-w0*t) dt.

Then RH is equivalent to positive semidefiniteness of BOTH

    H_N=[a_(i+j)]_(i,j=0..N),
    H_N^+=[a_(i+j+1)]_(i,j=0..N)

for EVERY N. Necessity is immediate under -g>=0. For sufficiency,
the Stieltjes moment theorem produces a positive measure with these
moments. The known signed density eta(dt)=-g(t)exp(-w0*t)dt has
an exponential moment for every delta<w0-1/2, and its moments obey
factorial growth bounds C*n!/(w0-1/2)^(n+1), after changing C.
The positive representing measure also has an exponential moment:
expand cosh(delta*t), use positivity and the even-moment bounds,
and then use exp(delta*t)<=2*cosh(delta*t) for t>=0.

Thus both measures have analytic moment generating functions near
zero, with the same Taylor coefficients. Laplace/Fourier uniqueness
forces equality. Hence eta is positive, so g<=0 everywhere by
continuity; the paper's converse gives RH.

This is a precise analytic route: prove all the displayed Hankel
quadratic forms using the PRIME-side g or equivalent xi identities.
The theta kernel's raw-moment Hankel inequalities in Section 1 do
not imply these transformed Hankel inequalities.

6. Complete-monotonicity equivalence and remaining gap
----------------------------------------------------
RH implies L is completely monotone on all w>0 via the nonnegative
Laplace density -g. Conversely global complete monotonicity gives
a positive representing measure by Bernstein's theorem. Tilt at
w0>1/2 and compare with the known signed density, using uniqueness;
this gives -g>=0 and hence RH.

First missing lemma: for the SPECIFIC theta kernel A, prove either
all the transformed Hankel inequalities at one fixed w0>1/2 or all
inequalities (-1)^n*d^n[(log xi(1/2+w))'/w^2]>=0 for every w>0.
Even the first nontrivial ratio inequality requires a property beyond
positivity of A. Substituting hypothetical real zero locations into
the partial fractions would be circular.

Concrete next analytic test: derive an xi-specific weighted-moment
bound w*Var_w(X)<=2*E_w(X). A proof must exploit the explicit theta
kernel, rather than generic exponential-tilt convexity. Separately,
compare the first transformed Hankel determinant

    a_0*a_2-a_1^2 >= 0

with the raw theta-moment determinants, explicitly retaining the
quotient terms from differentiation of log F. This can reveal the
extra covariance inequality that an analytic proof would need.

Writing mu=kappa'(w), v=kappa''(w), c3=kappa'''(w), direct
differentiation gives the exact first determinant formula

    a_0*a_2-a_1^2
      = [2*mu^2+w^2*(mu*c3-v^2)]/w^6.

Thus the first ordinary Hankel determinant asks for
2*mu^2+w^2*(mu*c3-v^2)>=0. Here mu and v are the tilted mean
and variance, while c3 is the tilted third central moment. Generic
theta-measure positivity gives v>0 but supplies no needed sign or
bound for this particular third-moment combination.
