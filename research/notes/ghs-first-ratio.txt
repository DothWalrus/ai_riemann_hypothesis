GHS and the first Laplace ratio inequality for xi
Bounded analytic batch, 2026-11-11.
Status: unconditional first-order result using a published xi-kernel
theorem; this is not complete monotonicity and does not establish RH.

Primary sources checked
-----------------------
1. C. M. Newman, The GHS inequality and the Riemann hypothesis,
Constructive Approximation 7 (1991), 389--399.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01888165

The publisher abstract explicitly states that for the even potential
V in the Riemann xi Fourier kernel, V' is convex on [0,infinity).
This is an unconditional theorem. The full article was not accessible
without payment in this batch; its stated result is sufficient below.

2. R. S. Ellis, J. L. Monroe, C. M. Newman, The GHS and other
correlation inequalities for a class of even ferromagnets,
Communications in Mathematical Physics 46 (1976), 167--182.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01608495

The publisher abstract confirms that even C^1 potentials with convex
V' belong to the class supporting their GHS inequalities. Instead of
relying on an uninspected multi-spin theorem, the necessary one-spin
implication is proved directly below.

Scaling and notation
--------------------
Use the positive theta kernel A from the companion note, with

    xi(1/2+w)=integral_0^infinity A(u) cosh(w*u) du.

The normalized symmetric density proportional to A(|x|) is exp(-W(x))
up to a constant, W=-log A. Newman's Fourier variable uses a positive
linear rescaling x=2t, so his potential is W(2t)+constant. Convexity
of the potential derivative is preserved by this rescaling. Therefore
W' is convex for x>=0. The theta kernel is smooth and positive; thus
W'' is even and nondecreasing as a function of |x|.

Let kappa(w)=log[xi(1/2+w)/xi(1/2)]. Under exponential tilt,
kappa'(w)=E_w X, kappa''(w)=Var_w X, and kappa'''(w) is the third
central moment. All integrations and differentiations below converge
because of the theta density's double-exponential tails.

Elementary one-site GHS implication
-----------------------------------
More generally let p_h(x)=Z(h)^(-1)*exp(h*x-W(x)), where W is even,
C^2, and W'' is nondecreasing on [0,infinity). Let h>0 and let
m=E_h X>0. For t>=0 define

    D(t)=log[p_h(m+t)/p_h(m-t)]
        =2*h*t-W(m+t)+W(m-t).

Then D(0)=0 and

    D''(t)=-W''(m+t)+W''(m-t)<=0,

since |m+t|>=|m-t|. Thus D is concave, D(t)/t is nonincreasing,
and q(t)=p_h(m+t)-p_h(m-t) has at most one sign change, from
positive to negative. The mean identity is

    integral_0^infinity t*q(t) dt = 0.

If q is not identically zero, this forces a crossing r in (0,infinity).
Then (t^3-r^2*t)*q(t)<=0 at every t, and integration gives

    E_h[(X-m)^3]=integral t^3*q(t)dt<=0.

The case q identically zero gives equality. Hence

    kappa'''(w)<=0 for EVERY w>=0.

At w=0 this also follows from symmetry. Applying the argument to
Newman's proven theta potential establishes this sign without any
assumption about the locations of xi zeros.

The requested ratio is therefore settled unconditionally
---------------------------------------------------------
Write mu(w)=kappa'(w)>0 and v(w)=kappa''(w)>0. Since v is
nonincreasing and mu(0)=0,

    mu(w)=integral_0^w v(s)ds >= w*v(w).

This is stronger than the requested w*kappa''<=2*kappa'. For
L(w)=kappa'(w)/w^2 it gives the explicit strict lower bound

    -L'(w)=[2*mu(w)-w*v(w)]/w^3
           >= mu(w)/w^3 >0,  w>0.

Thus L>0 and -L'>0 on the ENTIRE positive axis. These are only the
orders zero and one of complete monotonicity.

Other justified consequences
-----------------------------
With M=kappa''(0)>0,

    0<v(w)<=M,
    w*v(w)<=mu(w)<=M*w,
    mu(w)/w is nonincreasing,
    0<kappa(w)<=M*w^2/2,
    xi(1/2+w)/xi(1/2)<=exp(M*w^2/2).

The Taylor expansion of the nonincreasing ratio mu(w)/w also yields
kappa''''(0)<=0, equivalently

    xi''''(1/2)/xi(1/2)
       <=3*[xi''(1/2)/xi(1/2)]^2.

These conclusions exploit Newman's xi-specific kernel theorem.
They do not follow from positivity of a generic symmetric kernel.

Exact next unresolved inequalities
----------------------------------
GHS controls c3=kappa''' from ABOVE, while the next Laplace
conditions need LOWER bounds on it. In detail,

    L''(w)=[w^2*c3-4*w*v+6*mu]/w^4,

so second-order complete monotonicity requires

    w^2*c3 >= 4*w*v-6*mu.

The first ordinary Hankel determinant of the derivative moments is

    L*L''-(L')^2
      =[2*mu^2+w^2*(mu*c3-v^2)]/w^6.

It requires c3>=v^2/mu-2*mu/w^2. The sign c3<=0 by itself
establishes neither of these lower bounds. This is the precise
remaining moment inequality after resolving the first ratio.

Concrete next analytic test: seek a quantitative lower bound for
the tilted third central moment from the explicit theta potential,
with either of the two displayed lower bounds as the target.
An argument that merely repeats GHS or substitutes real zero
locations cannot supply the missing implication.
