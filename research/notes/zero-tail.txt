Finite zero-height verification and a bounded-time certificate
=============================================================

Result independently certified in this batch:

  g_zeta(t)<0 for 1/2<=t<=40.

Combining this with ns_startup_lemma.md gives g_zeta(t)<0 for
0<t<=40. This is a finite-range consequence of established numerical
theorems and elementary estimates, not a global proof or novelty claim.

2. Explicit tail bound, including both signs of the ordinates
-----------------------------------------------------------

For a zeta zero rho=beta+i tau, the corresponding zero gamma of
xi(1/2-i z) has |Re gamma|=|tau| and Im gamma=beta-1/2. Thus
|Im gamma|<1/2 and |gamma|^2>=tau^2. For t>=0,

 |exp(-i gamma t)-1|<=exp(t/2)+1.

Consequently, with all multiplicities included,

 |tail_H g(t)| <= (exp(t/2)+1) A(H),
 A(H)=sum_{|Re gamma|>H} multiplicity(gamma)/|gamma|^2
     <=2 sum_{tau>H} multiplicity(tau)/tau^2.

Stieltjes integration by parts gives

 sum_{tau>H} m_tau/tau^2
   =-N(H)/H^2+2 integral_H^infinity N(u)/u^3 du.

Substituting the lower bound at H and upper bound inside the integral
gives the explicit, unconditional estimate

 A(H) <= [log(H/(2*pi))+1]/(pi H)
          +[4R(H)+.11200+.12567/log H]/H^2.                (1)

The loglog integral is bounded using

 2 integral_H^infinity loglog(u)/u^3 du
 <=[loglog(H)+1/(2 log H)]/H^2.

The interval script obtains the upper bound

 A(3*10^12)<2.9594072618281164848*10^(-12)
           <2.959408*10^(-12).

For orientation only, the resulting absolute tail envelopes are about
0.001435802 at t=40, 0.01749165 at t=45, 0.06044436 at t=47.48,
and 0.213092 at t=50. The script uses the exact outward enclosures,
not these rounded orientation values.

3. Finite certificate from five zeros
------------------------------------

For any chosen verified positive real zeros tau_1,...,tau_m<=H, put

 D_m(t)=2 sum_{j=1}^m [1-cos(tau_j t)]/tau_j^2.

All other zeros below H also give nonpositive real contributions,
so the exact zero expansion implies

 g(t)<=-D_m(t)+(exp(t/2)+1)A(H).                           (2)

For the chosen five zeros,

 |D_5'(t)|<=L=2 sum_{j=1}^5 1/tau_j<0.443061.

On a cell [a,b], with midpoint c and radius r=(b-a)/2,

 D_5(t)>=D_5(c)-Lr,
 (exp(t/2)+1)A(H)<=(exp(b/2)+1)*2.959408*10^(-12).

Therefore a strictly positive lower interval bound for

 D_5(c)-Lr-(exp(b/2)+1)*2.959408*10^(-12)                  (3)

certifies g(t)<0 throughout the entire cell. The script bisects cells
until each passes (3). It evaluates cosine using directed arithmetic,
reduces the argument by an integer multiple of 2*pi, and uses its
Taylor polynomial through degree 60. The uniform remainder is bounded
by 4^62/62!, after asserting that the reduced argument is in [-4,4].
Pi is enclosed by Machin's formula; exp/ln receive the same one-ulp
enlargement as in ns_startup_certificate.py.

4. Exact obstruction to promoting a fixed finite prefix globally
--------------------------------------------------------------

For any fixed finite list of real frequencies, simultaneous
Diophantine approximation supplies arbitrarily large times at which
all phases tau_j t are arbitrarily close to multiples of 2*pi.
Consequently

 inf_{t>=T} D_m(t)=0 for every finite T.

This holds without assuming rational independence. A direct
pigeonhole argument gives, for each integer Q>=2, an integer
1<=q<=Q^m with ||q tau_j/(2*pi)||<=1/Q for every j, and then

 D_m(q)<=4*pi^2/Q^2 sum_j 1/tau_j^2.

If the selected q are unbounded this gives arbitrarily large returns;
if some q repeats as Q increases, that q is an exact common period
and its multiples give arbitrarily large exact returns. Thus no
strictly positive uniform deficit can be inferred from a finite
prefix, even if every zero below H were used.

Moreover D_m(t)<=4 sum_j 1/tau_j^2 is bounded, whereas the envelope
in (2) grows exponentially. For these five zeros its maximum possible
deficit is about 0.04347548; the envelope exceeds even that bound by
t approximately 46.821. Thus this particular five-zero/envelope
certificate cannot possibly cover all sufficiently large t, regardless
of subdivision accuracy. This is a limitation of the bound, not a
claim that g becomes positive.

The finite-height theorem can therefore support rigorous bounded-time
certificates. It cannot by itself rule out an off-line zero above H:
its contribution can remain tiny on every fixed bounded t interval
and eventually grow through the exp(|Im gamma|t) factor. The first
unresolved global step is controlling that high-zero contribution
uniformly for unbounded t, or obtaining independent structure that
prevents its positive excursions. No such global control is proved.
