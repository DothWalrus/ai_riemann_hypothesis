Its purpose is to say precisely what the complete-monotonicity route of
docs/RESEARCH_DIRECTION.md would have to prove, and where.

0. Summary
----------

Write, for an integer n >= 0 and real w > 0,

  c_n(w) = (-1)^n w^(n+1) L^(n)(w) / n!.

Complete monotonicity of L is "c_n(w) >= 0 for all n and all w > 0".

  (i)   For w > 1/2, c_n(w) is the average of -g against a Gamma
        probability density with mean (n+1)/w and variance (n+1)/w^2.
        The hierarchy is the pointwise sign of g seen through a
        smoothing window; it is not a separate route.

  (ii)  Given E1-E3, c_n(w) > 0 whenever w <= 9*10^24 and the window
        variance (n+1)/w^2 exceeds 8*T2 (standard deviation above
        3.45*10^-6). These inequalities hold whatever the zeros above
        the verified height do.

  (iii) Unconditionally, a zero rho_0 = 1/2 + b + i a with b > 0 forces
        c_n(w) < 0 for infinitely many n at EVERY real
        w > w*(rho_0) = (a^2 + b^2)/(2b). Below the least such
        threshold, c_n(w) tends to a positive constant as n grows.

  (iv)  Hence RH is equivalent to eventual sign-regularity at an
        unbounded set of w. By E1 the least threshold exceeds 9*10^24,
        and a heuristic count puts the first forced violation at orders
        of about 4*10^27 or more.

1. The hierarchy is a Gamma average of -g
-----------------------------------------

For w > 1/2 the transform recalled in laplace-moment-audit.txt is

  L(w) = int_0^infinity [-g(t)] e^(-w t) dt,

absolutely convergent because |g(t)| <= C (e^(t/2) + 1) unconditionally
(zero-tail.txt, Section 2). Differentiating n times under the integral,

  c_n(w) = int_0^infinity [-g(t)] p_{n,w}(t) dt,
  p_{n,w}(t) = w^(n+1) t^n e^(-w t) / n!,                     (w > 1/2).

p_{n,w} is the Gamma density with shape n+1 and rate w: total mass 1,
mean t_c = (n+1)/w, variance (n+1)/w^2 = t_c/w. Thus

  c_n(w) = E[-g(T)],   T ~ Gamma(n+1, w),   w > 1/2.                  (1.a)

Consistency with the zero side: for real tau,
E[2(1-cos(tau T))/tau^2] = (2/tau^2)[1 - Re (1 - i tau/w)^-(n+1)],
which is the on-line term of formula (1.1) multiplied by w^(n+1)/n!.

Post--Widder inversion. Let n -> infinity with (n+1)/w = t fixed. The
window narrows (relative width (n+1)^(-1/2)) and, g being continuous
with at most exponential growth, c_n((n+1)/t) -> -g(t). So

  c_n(w) >= 0 for all n and all w > 1/2   <=>   g(t) <= 0 for all t > 0.

("<=" is (1.a); "=>" is the limit.) This recovers the equivalence of
laplace-moment-audit.txt, Section 6, without Bernstein's theorem, and
shows which inequality carries which information: c_n(w) tests g near
t = (n+1)/w at resolution sqrt(t/w).

For 0 < w <= 1/2 the integral need not converge unconditionally, so
there c_n(w) is not asserted to be an average of g. It is still defined
through L and (1.1), which is all that Sections 2-4 use.

2. Lemma D: what finite verification decides
--------------------------------------------

For a zero rho with Im rho = a > 0 and Re rho = 1/2 + b, the two poles
of its term in (1.1) are at w' = +-p, p = b + i a (up to the labelling
of gamma), and

  |1 - p/w|^2 = 1 - 2b/w + (a^2+b^2)/w^2 = 1 - 2 b (w - w*)/w^2,
  w* = w*(rho) = (a^2+b^2)/(2b)   (b > 0),                            (2.a)
  |1 + p/w|^2 > 1.

So for an off-line zero with b > 0, |1 - p/w| >= 1 exactly when
w <= w*(rho); for its mirror zero (offset -b) both moduli exceed 1.
Consequently, by (1.1) multiplied by w^(n+1)/n!, every off-line zero
contributes to c_n(w) a term of modulus at most

  (2/|gamma|^2) (1 + 1) <= 4/a^2    whenever w <= w*(rho) for the
                                    quadruple it belongs to,          (2.b)

for every n >= 0. On-line zeros contribute nonnegative terms by (1.3),
at every height.

Let w_crit be the infimum of w*(rho) over zeros with Re rho > 1/2
(infinite under RH). Since 0 < b < 1/2 gives w*(rho) > a^2 + b^2, and
off-line zeros have a > H by E1,

  w_crit > H^2 = 9 * 10^24.                                           (2.c)

LEMMA D. Assume E1-E3 and let 0 < w <= 9*10^24. Then c_0(w) > 0, and
for every n >= 1 with

  n + 1 > 8 T2 w^2        (8 T2 < 1.184 * 10^-11)

one has c_n(w) > 0. In particular every order is positive for
w <= 4.1 * 10^5.

Proof. c_0 = w L = mu/w > 0. Let n >= 1, m = (n+1)/2 >= 1 and
x = tau_1^2/w^2. Split the zeros into on-line and off-line. Among the
on-line terms, all nonnegative, keep the one of tau_1 and use (1.3).
All off-line zeros have a > H, and w <= H^2 < w_crit, so (2.b) bounds
their total by 4 T2. Hence

  c_n(w) >= (2/tau_1^2) [1 - (1+x)^(-m)] - 4 T2.

Bernoulli's inequality (1+x)^m >= 1 + m x gives
1 - (1+x)^(-m) >= m x/(1 + m x). If m x >= 1 the bracket is at least
1/2 and c_n >= 1/tau_1^2 - 4 T2 > 0 (tau_1^2 < 200, 4 T2 < 6*10^-12).
If m x < 1 the bracket is at least m x/2 = (n+1) tau_1^2/(4 w^2), so
c_n >= (n+1)/(2 w^2) - 4 T2, positive under the hypothesis. Finally
n + 1 >= 2 > 8 T2 w^2 for w <= 4.1*10^5.                             QED

In the language of Section 1 (for w > 1/2): the hypothesis says the
window variance (n+1)/w^2 exceeds 8 T2, for which a standard deviation
above 3.45*10^-6 suffices. So, given E1-E3, every Gamma average of g
with rate 1/2 < w <= 9*10^24 and standard deviation above 3.45*10^-6
is negative. For a window centred at t the rate is t/(s.d.)^2, so the
rate restriction only matters for centres beyond about 10^14.

The proof shows more generally, with no hypothesis: if T_off denotes
the sum of 1/(Im rho)^2 over off-line zeros with Im rho > 0, then
c_n(w) > 0 whenever w <= w_crit and n + 1 > 8 T_off w^2.

The constants needed (T2 < 1.48*10^-12, 14.13 < tau_1 < 14.14) are
asserted by research/laplace_second_order.py; no new certificate is
required.

3. Theorem D: where an off-line zero must show itself
-----------------------------------------------------

L(w') = kappa'(w')/w'^2 is meromorphic in the plane. Its poles are
w' = 0 (simple, residue M = xi''(1/2)/xi(1/2) > 0, because kappa' is
odd and analytic at 0 with kappa'(w') ~ M w') and the points
w' = rho - 1/2 over zeros rho of xi. On the positive real axis L is
analytic, since xi(s) > 0 for real s.

THEOREM D (unconditional). Suppose zeta has a zero rho_0 with
Re rho_0 = 1/2 + b, b > 0, Im rho_0 = a. Then for every real
w > w*(rho_0) = (a^2 + b^2)/(2 b) there are infinitely many n with
c_n(w) < 0.

Proof. Fix w > w*. The Taylor series of y -> L(w - y) at y = 0 is
sum_n c_n(w) y^n / w^(n+1). Its radius of convergence R is the distance
from w to the nearest pole of L. By (2.a) the pole b + i a is at
distance less than w, so R < w. Suppose c_n(w) >= 0 for all n beyond
some n_0. Removing the first n_0 terms (a polynomial) leaves a power
series with nonnegative coefficients and the same radius R, so by the
Vivanti--Pringsheim theorem the point y = R is a singularity of
y -> L(w - y). But w - R lies in (0, w), on the positive real axis,
where L is analytic. Contradiction.                                   QED

COROLLARY. The following are equivalent.
  (1) RH.
  (2) c_n(w) > 0 for all n >= 0 and all real w > 0.
  (3) There is an unbounded set of real w such that, for each w in it,
      c_n(w) >= 0 for all sufficiently large n (how large may depend
      on w).

(1) => (2): under RH every zero is on the line, and (1.3) with
termwise differentiation gives strict positivity for every w > 0.
(2) => (3) is trivial. If RH fails, Theorem D produces a w* beyond
which (3) is violated at every w.

4. Proposition E: below the threshold the signs are eventually right
--------------------------------------------------------------------

PROPOSITION E (unconditional). For every real 0 < w < w_crit,

  c_n(w) -> M = xi''(1/2)/xi(1/2) > 0   as n -> infinity.

Proof. For an on-line zero the pole i tau is at distance
sqrt(w^2+tau^2) > w from w. For an off-line zero, (2.a) gives distance
greater than w because w < w_crit <= w*(rho); for its mirror the
distance always exceeds w. The squared distance minus w^2 equals
a^2 + b^2 - 2 b w >= a^2 - w, which tends to infinity along the zeros,
so the minimum over all zeros is attained and exceeds w. Hence
L(w - y) - M/(w - y) is analytic in a disc |y| < R' with R' > w, its
Taylor coefficients are O(r^-n) for some r > w, and those of M/(w - y)
are M/w^(n+1). Multiply by w^(n+1).                                  QED

Proposition E gives only eventual positivity: it does not exclude
finitely many negative c_n(w) at a given w < w_crit, and the threshold
in n is not explicit. The boundary case w = w_crit is covered by
neither statement. In Section 1's language, very wide windows at a
fixed rate below w_crit see the mean value M of -g.

5. The map
----------

Coordinates: order n >= 0 and parameter w > 0, or for w > 1/2 the
window centre t = (n+1)/w and variance t/w.

  A. DECIDED POSITIVE (Lemma D, given E1-E3):
       w <= 9*10^24 and (n = 0 or n + 1 > 1.184*10^-11 w^2).
     All orders for w <= 4.1*10^5. Independent of zeros above H.

  B. DECIDED POSITIVE at every w > 0 (laplace-second-order.txt):
       n = 2, and separately the first Hankel determinant (which is not
       a point of this plane). n = 0 and n = 1 are in ghs-first-ratio.txt.
     For other fixed n the same patching is expected to work and has
     not been carried out.

  C. NOT DECIDED by these notes:
       narrow windows, n + 1 <= 1.184*10^-11 w^2, at
       4.1*10^5 < w <= 9*10^24, other than n <= 2.
     Proposition E applies (c_n -> M at each such w), so no zero can
     force infinitely many violations here; finitely many are not
     excluded. For window centres t <= 40 the pointwise bound g < 0 on
     (0, 40] (zero-tail.txt) makes positivity very plausible, but
     turning that into a statement about c_n needs a tail estimate
     that has not been written.

  D. WHERE A COUNTEREXAMPLE IS FORCED TO APPEAR (Theorem D + (2.c)):
       w > 9*10^24.
     A zero at height a with offset b forces infinitely many negative
     c_n(w) at every w > (a^2+b^2)/(2b) > a^2 + b^2. Note these
     violations occur at arbitrarily large n, that is, at arbitrarily
     WIDE windows: at rates above the threshold the window average of
     the off-line oscillation grows instead of decaying.

What this does and does not say about "bounded ranges". Region A shows
that the inequalities with w <= H^2 and variance above 8 T2 hold
irrespective of zeros above H, so they cannot detect such zeros; with a
larger verified height the same holds on a larger range. For a bounded
range of n at all w the analogous statement is proved only for n <= 2.

6. How high must the order be in region D? (heuristic)
------------------------------------------------------

Not proved. By (1.1) and (2.a) the off-line pair's oscillating part of
c_n(w) has modulus

  (2/|p|^2) (1 - 2 b (w - w*)/w^2)^(-(n+1)/2)
     ~ (2/a^2) exp( b t (1 - w*/w) ),      t = (n+1)/w,

which grows only for w > w* and at the reduced rate b (1 - w*/w), not
b. (At w = w* it does not grow at all.) It must reach the main term,
of size M = 0.0462, so one needs roughly

  n ~ w^2 log(M a^2/2) / (b (w - w*)),

smallest at w = 2 w*, where n_min ~ 4 w* log(M a^2 / 2)/b. For a
hypothetical zero just above H:

     b        w*       w = 2 w*     n_min
    0.50   9.0e24      1.8e25      3.9e27
    0.10   4.5e25      9.0e25      9.7e28
    0.01   4.5e26      9.0e26      9.7e30

(output of research/postwidder_toy.py; b < 1/2 always, so the first
row is a limiting case). These numbers describe the formula. They are
not evidence about any zero. In the toy model of Section 7 the same
estimate predicts the observed first violation within a factor 1.5.

8. Consequences for the programme
---------------------------------

(1) "Route 2" of docs/RESEARCH_DIRECTION.md (complete monotonicity,
moment matrices) is "route 1" (the sign of g) viewed through Gamma
windows, by Section 1. Region A is the part of the hierarchy that
finite verification already settles, and by construction it is blind
to zeros above H. The author's opinion, not a theorem: the family
contains no inequality that is both outside region A and easier than
the corresponding statement about g, and the same is to be expected of
the higher Hankel determinants, which are positivity statements for
the same measure.

(2) Numerical experiments on c_n(w) inside region A cannot be evidence
for or against RH: their outcome is already determined. Experiments in
region C at reachable sizes have window centres t = (n+1)/w far below
40, where g < 0 is already certified pointwise, so they are not
expected to be informative either; that expectation is not a proof.

(3) What remains open is unchanged and is the statement recorded in
zero-tail.txt, Section 4, and chebyshev-error.txt: control of g(t), or
of the prime sum, uniformly for unbounded t. In the present language
two things are missing at once: windows narrower than 3.45*10^-6 at
large centres, and rates beyond H^2. Any proof would need an input
that distinguishes zeros above H, which none of the tools in this
repository does.

(4) Possible bounded follow-ups, in decreasing order of value:
  * Maintainer review of Sections 2-4.
  * Lean: Theorem D reduces to Pringsheim's theorem plus a statement
    about poles of xi'/xi; it is a cleaner formalization target than the
    interval certificates, with all number theory isolated in the
    hypothesis "rho_0 is a zero".
  * Write the tail estimate that would settle region C for window
    centres t <= 40.
  * Sharpen region A using all verified zeros instead of tau_1 alone.
    This changes constants, not the picture.
  * Not recommended: proving further fixed orders in region B.

Sources: Widder, The Laplace Transform (1941), for Post--Widder
inversion; Titchmarsh, The Theory of Functions, section 7.21, for the
Vivanti--Pringsheim theorem; the sources of laplace-second-order.txt.
