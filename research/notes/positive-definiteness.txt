Nakamura--Suzuki positive-definiteness and compound-Poisson audit
Independent research note, 2026-11-11. Source: supplied five-page PDF,
Statistics and Probability Letters 201 (2023), 109889.

Status: exact reformulations and one unconditional positivity lemma;
no proof of RH. Write g for the paper's prime-side function (1.5).

1. A scalar mass that is positive without RH

Set M = xi''(1/2)/xi(1/2). The functional equation gives xi'(1/2)=0.
The absolutely convergent reciprocal-square zero sum is

    M = sum_gamma m_gamma / gamma^2,

where gamma ranges over ALL zeros of xi(1/2-iz), including complex
ones if RH fails. This follows by differentiating the canonical product
twice at zero. Absolute convergence follows from the order-one zero
count. This identity does not assert that its summands are positive.

There is nevertheless an unconditional proof that M>0. The classical
theta integral, obtained from the Mellin transform and two integrations
by parts, is

    xi(1/2+z) = integral_0^infinity A(u) cosh(zu) du,
    A(u) = sum_{n>=1} (8*pi^2*n^4*exp(9u/2)
                      -12*pi*n^2*exp(5u/2))
                     *exp(-pi*n^2*exp(2u)).

Each summand of A is strictly positive for u>=0: factor it as
4*pi*n^2*exp(5u/2)*(2*pi*n^2*exp(2u)-3)*exp(-pi*n^2*exp(2u)).
The fast tail justifies differentiating under the integral. Consequently

    M = (integral u^2 A(u) du)/(integral A(u) du) > 0.

An ordinary, non-interval Simpson calculation gives M approximately
0.04620998623084, and exp(-M) approximately 0.95484143758322.
These decimal values are numerical checks, not certified bounds.

2. The exact constant-shift question

Let h_C(t)=C+g(t). If h_C is positive definite on R, its 2-by-2
matrices give h_C(t)<=h_C(0)=C, since h_C is real and even.
Thus g(t)<=0. The converse argument of the supplied paper then gives
RH. No presumption about real zero locations is needed for this step.

Under RH define the positive finite measure

    nu = sum_gamma (m_gamma/gamma^2) delta_{-gamma}.

Its total mass is M, and the paper's identity is g=hat(nu)-M.
Since gamma=0 is not a zero, nu({0})=0. Uniqueness of the Fourier
transform of finite signed measures proves that h_C is positive
definite exactly when C>=M. More precisely, for every real C,

    h_C is positive definite  <=>  [RH holds and C>=M].

In particular the natural zero-free candidate is h_M=M+g. A positive
value for M alone supplies none of its required matrix inequalities.

3. Conditional positivity and the first unresolved step

The usual sign convention calls psi=-g conditionally negative definite
if sum_{j,k} c_j conjugate(c_k) psi(t_j-t_k)<=0 whenever sum_j c_j=0.
Equivalently g is conditionally positive definite. The anchored kernel

    K(s,t)=g(s-t)-g(s)-g(t)

is positive semidefinite for every finite selection of points exactly
when g has this property. Under RH, K is the Gram kernel of
exp(-i*lambda*s)-1 in L^2(nu). Using that representation without RH
would assume precisely the real-zero support that is to be proved.

An explicit necessary two-point test for K is

    g(s)<=0, g(t)<=0,
    4*g(s)*g(t) >= [g(s-t)-g(s)-g(t)]^2.

The special zero-sum vector (1,-2,1) at (0,t,2t) requires
g(2t)>=4*g(t). These are concrete finite tests; passing every sampled
case does not establish the general criterion.

The first unresolved positivity step in this approach is proving
sum c_j conjugate(c_k) g(t_j-t_k)>=0 directly from the prime-side
formula for arbitrary finite zero-sum coefficients. Positive prime
weights do not justify it term by term. Indeed the ramp
r_a(t)=(|t|-a)_+ has quadratic form -2*r_a(t)<0 on coefficients
(1,-1) at (0,t) when |t|>a, which is the wrong sign. Prime and
archimedean terms therefore need a global cancellation argument.

The paper's converse also shows that exp(g) merely being a
characteristic function already implies RH: it implies g<=0.
Therefore, within this specific family, the following are equivalent:
RH; g<=0; -g conditionally negative definite; exp(g) positive definite;
exp(r*g) positive definite for every r>0; and h_M positive definite.
Infinite divisibility is a valid consequence, but the converse does
not require it.

4. Compound-Poisson probabilities: a common atom-counting trap

Under RH the probability measure is

    mu = exp(-M) * sum_{k>=0} nu^{*k}/k!.

Here exp(-M) is the probability of NO jumps. It is not mu({0}):
opposite jumps cancel because nu is symmetric. In fact

    mu({0}) = exp(-M)*sum_{k>=0} nu^{*k}({0})/k!
            >= exp(-M)*(1+sum_{gamma>0}(m_gamma/gamma^2)^2)
             > exp(-M).

The inequality already uses only the k=0 and k=2 contributions.
The distribution is purely atomic on the countable additive group
generated by the zero ordinates, though that group's closure need
not be discrete. Determining its exact zero atom using independent
coordinates would require controlling additive relations between
different positive ordinates; pair cancellation alone is sufficient
for the displayed strict lower bound.

5. Concrete next test

Evaluate the paper's PRIME-side g with interval error bounds, beginning
with the two-point K determinant above over a grid in (s,t), and add
the (1,-2,1) test. Include differences crossing log(p^k), because the
prime ramps turn on there. For larger finite point sets, test the
smallest eigenvalue of K with rigorous rounding/error control and
record the minimizing zero-sum vector. A negative certified value
would contradict RH through the paper's equivalence; positive finite
tests only characterize where a prospective all-points inequality
must obtain its cancellation. An analytic proof of those general
inequalities remains unresolved.
